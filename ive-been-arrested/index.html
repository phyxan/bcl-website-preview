I Have Been Arrested in Dallas. Now What?

Peter M. Barrett, Attorney Licensed in Texas since 1994 · 150+ jury trials · Dallas, Texas
✓ Statutes checked Texas codes verified September 2026
Straight Answer

What should I do after being arrested in Dallas?

Three things. Say the one sentence that protects you: "I want a lawyer, and I am not answering questions." Do not explain, even to be polite. Then use the process: Texas law requires you to be brought before a magistrate within 48 hours (Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 15.17), where your rights are read and bail is usually addressed. And get a criminal defense lawyer moving before that hearing, because bond and the first statements are decided early.

TL;DR
  • Stop talking. Anything explained in the car or the interview room becomes evidence.
  • The magistrate hearing happens within 48 hours. Bail is usually set there.
  • Phone calls from jail are recorded. Talk logistics, never the case.
  • A lawyer can be working on bond and evidence before the first hearing.
  • If you are reading this for someone in custody, note their full name and where they are held, then call a lawyer, then a bondsman if needed.

An arrest is designed to be disorienting, and every decision in the first hours gets made under pressure. This page is the short version of what matters tonight. The rights that actually matter tonight

You have the right to remain silent and the right to a lawyer. Neither works automatically: you have to invoke them, out loud, clearly. "I want a lawyer" ends questioning in a way that staying quiet alone does not. Officers may still talk to you, be friendly, or suggest that explaining will help. It will not, and you will not talk your way out of a booking that has already happened. Our guides to Miranda rights and using the right to remain silent explain what is and is not protected.

Two rules for the phone in jail: calls are recorded, and recordings get played to juries. Use calls for logistics, names and bond money, never for what happened.

The 48 hour rule

Under art. 15.17, whoever holds you must take you before a magistrate without unnecessary delay, and no later than 48 hours after arrest. The magistrate tells you the accusation, reads your rights, takes your request for appointed counsel if you cannot afford one, and in most cases addresses bail. This hearing is short and it matters: the bail number set there decides whether you wait for your case at home or in a cell. What moves that number is covered in how bail is decided.

If someone you love was arrested

  • Find them. For most of the county, that is the Dallas County jail system; suburbs book into city jails first. You need their full legal name and date of birth.
  • Call a lawyer before you spend on a bond. In some cases a lawyer can get a bond lowered, or arranged as a personal bond, before money changes hands.
  • Do not relay case details by phone or in visits. The recording rule applies to both directions.
  • Move early. The window between arrest and the first court settings is when release conditions, evidence preservation and charging decisions are still fluid.

What happens after release

A bond comes with conditions, court dates you cannot miss, and a case that now needs a defense. Missing a setting creates a new warrant on top of the original charge. What the charge itself carries, from a Class C fine to a first degree felony, is set out in Texas misdemeanors and felonies, and no plea of guilty can even be accepted by a court unless it is made freely, voluntarily and with the required warnings under art. 26.13. The decision of how to plead deserves its own page: pleading guilty or not guilty.

Common questions

How long can they hold me before I see a judge in Texas?

Article 15.17 requires you to be taken before a magistrate without unnecessary delay, and not later than 48 hours after arrest. That hearing is where your rights are read and bail is usually addressed.

Should I answer police questions if I am innocent?

No. Innocent people talk themselves into charges every week, because explanations get compared against evidence you have not seen. Say you want a lawyer and stop there. Innocence is proved through the case, not the interview room.

Can I get out of jail before seeing the magistrate?

Sometimes. In some situations a bond amount is available from a schedule before the hearing, and a lawyer or bondsman can start immediately. Otherwise the magistrate sets bail at the article 15.17 hearing, which is another reason to have counsel moving early.

Are jail phone calls really recorded?

Yes, and they are used. Assume every call and visit is on the record except conversations with your lawyer, which are privileged. Keep every other conversation to logistics.

What does hiring a lawyer in the first day actually change?

Bond arguments at the first hearing, a voice telling investigators questioning is over, preservation letters for video that gets erased on short cycles, and sometimes contact with the prosecutor before charges are formally filed. All of that is gone by the first court setting.
