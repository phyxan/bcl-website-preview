What Happens to Your Car Insurance After a DWI

Peter M. Barrett, Attorney Licensed in Texas since 1994 · 150+ jury trials · Dallas, Texas
✓ Statutes checked Texas codes verified September 2026
Straight Answer

What happens to car insurance after a DWI in Texas?

Two separate things. The State makes you prove coverage: reinstating your licence after a DWI suspension requires an SR-22 certificate filed by your insurer with DPS and maintained for two years, at the state minimums of $30,000 per person, $60,000 per crash for injury and $25,000 for property damage. And the market reprices you: filing an SR-22 tells your insurer about the conviction, and insurers surcharge or non-renew from there. There is no fixed increase; each company prices it.

TL;DR
  • An SR-22 is not insurance. It is your insurer certifying to DPS that you are insured, filed for two years.
  • If the policy lapses, the insurer must tell DPS, and the licence suspension comes back.
  • No car? A non-owner SR-22 policy satisfies the filing.
  • The rate damage fades with a clean record, and sealing the conviction later helps everything except the insurer that already knows.
  • The cheapest insurance strategy is a defense strategy: no conviction, no SR-22.

The insurance bill is the part of a DWI that keeps arriving after everything else is resolved. Here is how the machinery actually works in Texas, what is required by the State versus chosen by insurers, and where the exits are. Peter M. The SR-22, what it actually is

An SR-22 is a form, not a policy: a certificate of financial responsibility your insurance company files with the Texas Department of Public Safety certifying that you carry at least the state minimum liability coverage. DPS requires it to reinstate driving privileges after qualifying suspensions, DWI suspensions included, and per DPS guidance the filing must be maintained for two years from the date of the conviction that required it.

The coverage behind it must meet Texas minimums: $30,000 for injury to one person, $60,000 for injury to two or more, $25,000 for property damage. Two mechanical points people learn the hard way: not every insurer files SR-22s, so you sometimes have to switch companies just to get the form filed. And if the policy lapses or is cancelled during the two years, the insurer is required to notify DPS, which reinstates the suspension. A missed payment quietly becomes a suspended licence, and driving on it is a new criminal charge.

No vehicle in your name? A non-owner SR-22 policy covers you as a driver rather than a car and satisfies the filing. It is also usually the cheapest route through the two years for people who can live without owning a car. An occupational licence during a suspension likewise requires the SR-22 on file first.

What insurers do with it

The SR-22 filing and the conviction on your motor vehicle record are how your insurer finds out. From there, the responses range from a surcharge, to loss of good-driver discounts, to non-renewal, and pricing varies enough between companies that the spread matters more than any average. Older versions of this article quoted specific percentage increases; there is no official figure, so treat any exact number you read as one company's snapshot. What is consistent:

  • the conviction affects pricing for as long as it shows on the record insurers rate on, commonly the three to five year window, with the first renewal after conviction being the painful one;
  • shopping the market matters more after a DWI than at any other time, because carriers price the same conviction very differently;
  • the state fine under Transp. Code §709.001 ($3,000 to $6,000) and the suspension under §521.344 are separate costs, set by law rather than by your insurer. The full cost stack is in the effects of a Texas DWI.

Containing the damage

  • Fight the case first. Every insurance consequence hangs off the conviction. A dismissal, an acquittal or a reduction to a non-DWI offense changes the whole cascade. The defense angles are in the complete DWI defense guide, and the 15 day ALR deadline is where the licence fight starts.
  • Do not let the SR-22 lapse. Autopay the policy for the full two years. The lapse notification to DPS is automatic.
  • Re-shop at each renewal. The market's memory fades on a schedule; your current carrier's does not have to.
  • Seal the record when eligible. Many first DWI convictions can later be sealed by nondisclosure, which cleans up employment and housing checks. Insurers rating off driving records are the exception, but the rest of life improves: see expunction vs nondisclosure.

Common questions

How long do I need an SR-22 in Texas after a DWI?

Two years from the date of the conviction that required it, per DPS. The filing must stay continuously in force; a lapse is reported to DPS and revives the suspension.

Is an SR-22 the same as high risk insurance?

No. It is a certificate your insurer files with DPS proving you carry at least minimum coverage. The policy behind it can be an ordinary one; the filing is what is special, and not every company offers it.

How much does insurance go up after a DWI in Texas?

There is no official or fixed figure; each insurer prices the conviction its own way, which is why quotes after a DWI vary so widely. The reliable levers are shopping multiple carriers, keeping the record otherwise clean, and re-quoting at every renewal.

Do I need an SR-22 if I do not own a car?

If you want your licence back during the filing period, yes. A non-owner SR-22 policy covers you as a driver and satisfies the DPS requirement, usually at lower cost than insuring a vehicle.

Will my insurer find out about my DWI if I do not tell them?

Assume yes. The SR-22 filing itself involves your insurer, and the conviction appears on the motor vehicle record insurers pull at renewal. Hiding it is not a strategy; timing renewals and shopping carriers is.
