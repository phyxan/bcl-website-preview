How to Avoid a Probation Violation in Texas

Peter M. Barrett, Attorney Licensed in Texas since 1994 · 150+ jury trials · Dallas, Texas
✓ Statutes checked Texas codes verified September 2026
Straight Answer

What counts as a probation violation in Texas, and who decides?

Breaking any condition in your supervision order: a missed appointment, a failed or missed test, unpaid fees, leaving the county without permission, or a new offense. Your probation officer reports it, the State files a motion to revoke or adjudicate, and a judge decides, at a hearing without a jury under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 42A.751, where the State's burden is a preponderance of the evidence, not proof beyond a reasonable doubt.

TL;DR
  • The conditions are written in your order. Get a copy, read it, and calendar everything in it.
  • One violation rarely revokes anyone. Silence and disappearance after a violation is what revokes people.
  • If you are arrested and held on a violation, the hearing must happen within 20 days of the motion.
  • The judge's options run from continuing you unchanged to the full original sentence, and on deferred adjudication, the full punishment range.
  • A lawyer at the hearing can contest the violation, the proof, and above all the consequence.

Most revocations are not caused by a dramatic new crime. They are caused by drift: a missed appointment that becomes three, unpaid fees that become an unreturned phone call, a move nobody reported. This page covers the rules that actually trip people, what happens mechanically after a violation, and how to recover from a slip before it becomes a warrant. Peter M. The conditions that actually trip people

Judges set conditions under art. 42A.104, and the standard set is long. These are the ones that generate most motions to revoke, and what keeps each one manageable:

ConditionHow it gets violatedWhat keeps you safe
Report to your officerMissed or late appointments, unreported address or job changesCalendar every date; report changes in writing within days, not weeks
TestingA failed test, or a missed one, which counts the sameNever skip a test; a dirty test with honesty beats a no-show
Fees, fines, restitutionFalling behind and going silentPay something every month and ask for a payment review before you default
Travel limitsLeaving the county or state without written permissionRequest permission in advance, in writing, every time
Commit no new offenseAny new arrest, including a traffic-stop discoveryThe one condition with no paperwork fix; a new charge means call your lawyer that day
Programs and classesDropping out of treatment, DWI education, anger managementFinish early where possible; completion certificates end arguments
No contact ordersContacting a protected person, even by text or through friendsNo contact means none, including replies

What happens after a violation, step by step

  1. The officer reports. Your probation officer documents the violation. Officers have discretion: minor slips with good communication often end in a warning or an internal sanction rather than a report.
  2. The State files. A motion to revoke community supervision, or to adjudicate on deferred, is filed by the prosecutor. Only the State can file it, and only the judge can grant it.
  3. Warrant or summons. The court can issue a summons to appear or a warrant. On some technical violations the statute points to a summons first, but plan for either. An outstanding motion does not expire.
  4. Detention and the 20 day rule. If you are arrested and held on the violation, art. 42A.751 entitles you to a hearing on the alleged violation within 20 days of the motion being filed.
  5. The hearing. Before the judge, without a jury. The State proves the violation by a preponderance of the evidence, the standard Texas courts apply at revocation, and you have the right to counsel, to testify and to call witnesses.
  6. The decision. The judge may continue, extend, modify, or revoke the supervision. Revocation is one option, not the default.

What you are actually risking

Your situationIf revoked, the exposure is
Straight probation after convictionUp to the original sentence. Under art. 42A.755 the judge disposes of the case as if there had been no supervision, or can reduce the term, but not below the offense minimum
Deferred adjudicationThe full punishment range for the offense, because no sentence was ever assessed. A ten year deferred on a first degree felony can end in a life-range sentencing hearing
Time already served on supervisionGenerally does not count against the sentence

That middle row is the one people misunderstand, and it is why the plea decision and the supervision type matter so much at the front of a case: see pleading guilty or not guilty and community supervision explained.

If you have already slipped

  • Do not disappear. Absconding converts a fixable technical violation into the State's easiest case. Contact, honesty and a paper trail are the raw material your lawyer works with.
  • Fix what is fixable before the hearing. Enroll back in the class, catch up what you can on fees, get the missed test rescheduled. Judges respond to trajectory.
  • Do not wait for the warrant. If a motion is coming, a lawyer can sometimes arrange a walk-through and bond, argue for a summons, or negotiate a modification instead of a revocation: sanctions short of prison exist precisely for this. See can you violate probation and not go to jail?
  • Treat a new arrest as two cases. The new charge and the revocation motion move on different standards in different courts. Defending them together, deliberately, is the job.

Common questions

Who decides whether I violated probation?

The judge. Your probation officer reports and recommends, the prosecutor files the motion, but only the court decides, after a hearing under article 42A.751 where you have the right to a lawyer, to testify and to call witnesses.

Can I go to jail for a first technical violation?

You can, but it is uncommon. The judge's menu runs from continuing supervision unchanged, through added conditions or an extension, to revocation. Communication, quick repair and counsel at the hearing are what keep a first slip in the lower part of that menu.

How long can they hold me before a revocation hearing?

If you are detained on the violation, article 42A.751 requires the hearing within 20 days of the motion being filed. A lawyer can also seek release on bond in the meantime, depending on the case.

What is the burden of proof at a revocation hearing?

A preponderance of the evidence, before a judge without a jury. That is a lower bar than a criminal trial, which is why revocation defense leans on disproving the specific allegation, showing the violation was not wilful, and arguing the consequence.

Does my time on probation count if I am revoked?

Generally no. Article 42A.755 says the period on community supervision is not credited against the sentence, with narrow exceptions. Years of compliance do not buy down the exposure, which is one more reason to fight the revocation itself.
