Navigating Through Your Courtroom Appearance

Peter M. Barrett, Attorney 31 years practicing · 150+ jury trials · Dallas, Texas
Straight Answer

How should you prepare for a criminal court appearance?

Arrive early, dress as you would for a business interview, and let your attorney do the talking. Court time is limited and as the defendant you will rarely be given the chance to explain your charge, so how you present yourself is most of the impression the judge, opposing counsel and jury will form. If the judge asks you something directly, answer briefly.

TL;DR
  • Plan to arrive a few minutes early so you can go over last minute details with your attorney.
  • Dress professionally. As the defendant you will seldom be invited to speak, so appearance carries the impression.
  • Let your attorney speak for you. Court is not the place to declare your innocence yourself.
  • If the judge asks you a direct question, keep the answer brief and concise.
  • Ask your attorney before you go in what to say and when to say it.

If you’ve been arrested and facing criminal charges, you’ll have to make an appearance in criminal court. Because time is limited during a courtroom appearance, it’s imperative that you prepare adequately to ensure you make the most of the time you are given. In this blog, our Dallas criminal defense attorney shares a few tips on how to prepare for an upcoming courtroom appearance.

Be Punctual

You want to show your lawyer, the judge, and the jury that you value their time as much as they value yours. Showing up late or not showing up at all shows a lack of respect. You should plan on arriving to your appearance a few minutes early to go over any last minute details with your attorney.

Dress to Impress

Think of your court appearance as a business interview – you want to dress in a way that is professional and appropriate. Because you are the defendant, the chances of you being given the opportunity to speak
or explain your charge is very slim. How you dress is the only impression you will give to the judge, opposing counsel, and jury.

Think Before Speaking

While you may see this as an opportunity to declare your innocence, it’s important that you let your attorney do the talking. If you are asked a question directly by the judge, you should answer in a way that is brief and concise. Before stepping into the courtroom, you should be instructed by your attorney on what to say and when to say it.

Common questions

Will I get to explain my side at a court appearance?

Rarely. Time in the courtroom is limited and as the defendant the chance of being invited to speak or explain the charge is very slim. Your attorney speaks for you.

What should I wear to criminal court in Dallas?

Treat it as a business interview and dress professionally and appropriately. How you dress is often the only impression you give the judge, opposing counsel and jury.

What happens if I am late to my court appearance?

Showing up late, or not at all, reads as a lack of respect for the court's time. Plan to arrive a few minutes early so you can speak with your attorney before you go in.
