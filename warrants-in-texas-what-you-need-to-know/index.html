Warrants in Texas: What You Need to Know

Peter M. Barrett, Attorney Licensed in Texas since 1994 · 150+ jury trials · Dallas, Texas
✓ Statutes checked Texas codes verified September 2026
Straight Answer

What is a warrant in Texas?

A written order from a judge or magistrate. An arrest warrant under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 15.01 commands an officer to take a named person into custody. A search warrant under art. 18.01 authorises a search of a specific place, and it issues only on a sworn affidavit showing probable cause. A capias issues after a case is already filed, and a bench warrant is the court ordering you brought in, most often after a missed appearance. A Texas warrant does not expire.

TL;DR
  • Arrest warrant: issued on a probable cause affidavit before or at the start of a case.
  • Bench warrant and capias: issued by the court in an existing case, usually for a missed setting or a bond problem.
  • Search warrant: authorises a search, not an arrest, and stands or falls on the affidavit behind it.
  • Warrants do not expire, and traffic tickets left unanswered turn into them.
  • Walking in with a lawyer and a plan beats being arrested at work or at a traffic stop. In many cases bond can be arranged first.

Most people find out about a warrant the wrong way: at a traffic stop, at the airport, or from a bondsman's postcard. The four kinds of warrant you will actually meet

Arrest warrant. Art. 15.01 defines it: a written order from a magistrate, directed to a peace officer, commanding him to take the body of the person accused of an offense. Under art. 15.02 it must name you or describe you, state the offense you are accused of, and be signed by the magistrate. Behind every valid arrest warrant sits a sworn complaint or affidavit establishing probable cause. That affidavit is where a defense starts, because a warrant built on a defective affidavit can take the evidence that followed it down too.

Bench warrant. Issued by the judge from the bench in a case that already exists, most often because someone missed a court date. It adds a new problem on top of the old one: the missed appearance can be charged separately, and the judge who set the original bond now has a reason to raise it.

Capias. Under art. 23.01, a writ issued by a judge of the court having jurisdiction after a case is filed, commanding an officer to arrest the person and bring them before the court. In practice you see a capias after an indictment, or when a bond is revoked or forfeited.

Search warrant. Different animal. Under art. 18.01(b), no search warrant issues unless facts presented to the magistrate establish probable cause, and a sworn affidavit setting out substantial facts must be filed in every instance. It authorises a search of a described place for described things. If officers show up with one, you do not have to consent to anything beyond it, and nothing requires you to answer questions while they execute it. See when police can search without a warrant.

Where warrants come from

  • a new criminal charge, after a detective's affidavit is signed by a magistrate;
  • a missed court appearance, including on an ordinary traffic ticket;
  • an unpaid fine or a broken payment plan on a Class C case;
  • a motion to revoke probation or to adjudicate, which carries its own warrant;
  • a bond violation, such as a failed test or a missed check-in, ending in a capias.

The traffic ticket route surprises people most. A Class C citation you never answered quietly becomes two warrants in many courts: one for the ticket, one for the failure to appear. They sit there until a licence renewal, a traffic stop or an airport check brings them up.

How to find out if you have a warrant

  • County records. Most Texas counties, Dallas County included, let you search active warrants or pending cases through the sheriff's department or the county clerk. Municipal courts keep their own lists for Class C warrants.
  • A lawyer can check for you. This is the safest route, because a defense lawyer can look without putting you in a lobby where the warrant can be executed on the spot.
  • Do not call the police to ask about yourself. You may get your answer in person.

What happens when a warrant is executed

After an arrest, art. 15.17 requires that you be taken before a magistrate without unnecessary delay, and not later than 48 hours after arrest. The magistrate must tell you the accusation, your right to remain silent, your right to counsel and your right to an examining trial, and must make a bail decision if the law allows one. What you say between the handcuffs and that hearing is evidence, which is why the only sentence worth saying is that you want a lawyer. How a warrant actually gets cleared

  • Surrender on your terms. For most warrants, a lawyer can arrange a walk-through: bond ready before you appear, so the time in custody is minutes rather than days. Compare that with being taken at work on a Friday afternoon.
  • Class C warrants can often be lifted by posting a bond or appearing in the municipal court, without an arrest at all.
  • Bench warrants and capias need the underlying problem fixed: the missed setting reset, the bond reinstated or replaced. How bail gets set and argued is covered in how bail is decided.
  • Challenge the warrant itself. If the affidavit did not establish probable cause, that failure follows the case, and evidence found during the arrest or search can be suppressed.

Common questions

Do warrants expire in Texas?

No. An arrest warrant, bench warrant or capias stays active until it is executed or withdrawn. Warrants from years ago still stop licence renewals and still turn traffic stops into arrests.

Can I just pay off a warrant?

Only the Class C fine-only kind, and even then paying resolves the ticket by convicting you of it, which is not always the smart move. For anything above a Class C, the warrant clears through surrender and bond, not payment.

Will I be arrested at the airport over a warrant?

It happens. Airport and border checks query the same databases a patrol officer uses. A felony warrant will very likely be acted on anywhere in the country; whether a distant county comes to get you on a misdemeanor varies, but the arrest itself still happens.

How fast do I see a judge after a warrant arrest?

Article 15.17 requires it without unnecessary delay and not later than 48 hours after arrest. The magistrate informs you of the accusation and your rights, and makes a bail decision if the law allows one.

Should I turn myself in without a lawyer?

You can, but you give up the two things worth having: a bond arranged before you walk in, and someone making sure you answer no questions. A planned surrender usually means less time in custody and no statement to undo later.
