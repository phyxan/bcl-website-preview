Case CommentaryNorth Texas cases, explained

The Law Behind the Headline

A news story tells you what happened. It rarely tells you what the charge actually requires the State to prove, or where the real fight in the case will be. These are attorney breakdowns of North Texas cases already in the public record, written for people trying to understand how Texas criminal law works in practice.

How to read this section

Every article here discusses a case this firm is not involved in, using only facts already reported by news outlets or contained in public court records. A criminal charge is an accusation, not a finding. Everyone charged is presumed innocent until the State proves guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. Nothing here predicts how any case will end, and nothing here is legal advice about yours.

Why this section exists

A charge is a legal claim, not a story

When a serious case hits the news in Dallas, the coverage moves fast and stops at the surface: what was charged, what the bond was set at, what a witness saw. That leaves the questions people actually search for unanswered. What does the State have to prove to convict? What does the punishment range really look like? Does a plea help? What can a defense lawyer still do once blood has already been drawn?

Those questions have real answers in the Texas Penal Code and the Code of Criminal Procedure. This section works through them one case at a time, citing the statutes so you can check the work.
